About this site

October 18, 2026

This site is about one thing: once an AI agent is running in production, how do you know it did the right thing, what it cost, and where it got stuck. Not model reviews, not prompt tricks — observability, cost and operations, the problems that only show up after the demo ships.

What we write about

  • Observability: how trace, metrics and evaluation apply to agents, and why traditional APM cannot see "did the wrong thing".
  • Trace semantics: what an agent's behaviour tree actually looks like, and how to read the OpenTelemetry GenAI conventions.
  • Evaluation: from rule-based scoring to LLM-as-a-Judge, and how to give "right vs. wrong" a reproducible score.
  • Cost: where the tokens go, how a bill runs away quietly, and how self-hosting compares with per-token pricing.
  • Tooling: the real landscape of observability products and the trade-offs of wiring them end to end.

Who it is for

People shipping agents to production: the ones who have to explain why this month's bill tripled, answer "why did it get that wrong yesterday", and pick between options while owning the consequences. The writing assumes you can read logs and code; it does not assume you have already hit every trap.

How it is written

As first-hand and reproducible as possible: a real trace structure instead of a sketch, a concrete formula instead of "costs rise significantly". Where provider capabilities and prices are involved, the official documentation is the source and the date is noted — numbers in this field move fast, and a six-month-old conclusion may no longer hold.

No paid placements and no sponsored rankings. If sponsored content ever appears, it will be labelled as such.

Relationship to the tools site

The sister site tools.ezaiops.com hosts free tools that run entirely in your browser: LLM cost estimation, model price comparison, subnet and cron calculators, a JWT debugger and more. A lot of the arithmetic in these posts was done there.

Who maintains it

ezaiops. Source and issues live on GitHub; corrections and topic suggestions are welcome — the fastest route is the contact page.
